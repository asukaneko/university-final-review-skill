# Discipline Problem-Solving Coach

Use this module when the course requires more than memorization: calculation, proof, experiment interpretation, legal case analysis, clinical reasoning, data interpretation, essay planning, design critique, or other discipline-specific problem solving.

## Purpose

This module generalizes the old calculation / algorithm workflow into a cross-disciplinary problem-solving framework. The assistant should first identify the discipline category, then choose the correct reasoning template.

## Universal output structure

For each problem type, include:

1. Problem type name
2. Applicable discipline and exam scenario
3. Required concepts, rules, formulas, methods, or frameworks
4. Step-by-step solving or analysis process
5. Worked example
6. Common variations
7. Mistake-prone points
8. Fast exam judgment method
9. Practice questions
10. Detailed answers or model outlines

## Discipline-specific templates

### STEM / Engineering

Use for calculations, derivations, proofs, algorithms, code reading, system design, and experiment analysis.

Include:

- Known conditions and target result
- Formula, theorem, algorithm, or design rule
- Step-by-step derivation or execution trace
- Units, assumptions, and boundary conditions
- Complexity, error, or performance analysis when relevant

### Natural Sciences / Mathematics

Use for theorem proofs, formula derivations, mechanism explanations, graph interpretation, and experimental analysis.

Include:

- Definitions, assumptions, and variables
- Law / theorem / model selection
- Derivation, proof, or mechanism chain
- Data, graph, or experimental evidence interpretation
- Error sources and limitations

### Medicine / Health

Use for clinical cases, disease mechanisms, drug reasoning, nursing decisions, public-health indicators, and prevention strategies.

Include:

- Key symptoms, signs, indicators, and risk factors
- Mechanism or pathophysiology chain
- Diagnostic or classification logic
- Treatment, prevention, nursing, or public-health principles
- Safety cautions and exam traps

Frame outputs as study support only, not professional medical advice.

### Law

Use for legal hypotheticals, case briefs, doctrine comparison, and rule application.

Prefer IRAC or a similar structure:

- Issue
- Rule
- Application
- Conclusion

Also include rule elements, exceptions, burden or standard when relevant, and common traps.

### Humanities

Use for essay questions, textual interpretation, historical explanation, and compare-and-contrast prompts.

Include:

- Thesis statement
- Context
- Evidence or textual details
- Analysis
- Comparison or counterargument
- Conclusion outline

### Social Sciences

Use for theory application, research-method questions, case analysis, policy analysis, and data interpretation.

Include:

- Theory or concept
- Method, variable, evidence, or case
- Analysis chain
- Alternative explanation
- Limitation
- Policy or social implication when relevant

### Business / Economics

Use for model application, financial calculation, accounting entries, graph analysis, and case decision-making.

Include:

- Model or framework
- Assumptions
- Calculation or case facts
- Interpretation
- Decision recommendation
- Risks and limitations

Frame outputs as study support only, not financial or business advice.

### Education / Arts / Design

Use for lesson-plan analysis, pedagogy application, artwork critique, design critique, portfolio reflection, and creative process explanation.

Include:

- Theory, style, method, or design principle
- Context and objective
- Evidence from the lesson, work, or project
- Analysis and critique
- Improvement suggestions or reflection outline

## Output principle

Do not force all courses into calculation or algorithm formats. Choose the reasoning pattern that matches the discipline and exam task.
