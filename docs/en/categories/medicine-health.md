# Medicine / Health

Use this guide for medicine, nursing, pharmacy, dentistry, public health, epidemiology, anatomy, physiology, pathology, pharmacology, clinical skills, and allied health courses.

## Safety boundary

Outputs are for academic study only. Do not present generated content as medical advice, diagnosis, treatment instruction, or a substitute for professional guidance. Flag uncertainty and encourage verification against course materials, guidelines, and instructor-approved sources.

## Review goal

Help the student connect normal structure/function, disease mechanisms, clinical presentation, investigation, management principles, medication safety, public-health reasoning, and exam-style clinical thinking.

## What to extract from materials

- Anatomy, physiology, pathology, microbiology, pharmacology, and clinical-skill frameworks.
- Disease definitions, etiologies, risk factors, mechanisms, signs/symptoms, red flags, investigations, and management principles.
- Drug classes, mechanisms, indications, contraindications, adverse effects, interactions, monitoring, and patient counseling points.
- Clinical cases, OSCE checklists, nursing care plans, epidemiological measures, and public-health interventions.
- Diagrams, tables, pathways, lab values, imaging descriptions, and guideline summaries provided by the course.

## Chapter note structure

1. **Core system or condition overview**.
2. **Normal baseline**: anatomy/physiology or public-health context.
3. **Pathophysiology/mechanism**: causal chain from risk factor to manifestation.
4. **Presentation**: key symptoms, signs, red flags, and differential clues.
5. **Investigations**: what each test checks and how to interpret patterns.
6. **Management principles**: academic summary of options, priorities, and safety concerns.
7. **Pharmacology table**: mechanism, use, adverse effects, contraindications, monitoring.
8. **Case reasoning template**: assessment, priority, rationale, next step.
9. **Common mistakes**: confusing similar conditions, missing red flags, unsafe assumptions.
10. **Practice cases and short-answer questions**.

## Typical exam question types

- Anatomy/physiology labeling and mechanism explanation.
- Compare similar diseases, drugs, organisms, or public-health measures.
- Interpret lab values, vital signs, epidemiological data, or case vignettes.
- Explain pathophysiology from cause to symptom.
- Pharmacology mechanism/adverse-effect/contraindication questions.
- Nursing priorities, patient education, ethics, safety, and escalation.
- OSCE-style station planning or clinical communication.

## Clinical reasoning template

Use: **Problem representation → Key findings → Differentials → Most likely explanation → Investigations → Priorities → Safety/red flags → Patient-centered explanation**.

For each case, separate:

- What is given directly.
- What is inferred.
- What cannot be concluded from the information.
- What would be unsafe to assume.

## Public health / epidemiology template

Use: **Population → Exposure/intervention → Outcome → Measure → Bias/confounding → Interpretation → Policy implication**.

Include formulas for incidence, prevalence, risk ratio, odds ratio, sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV, and confidence intervals when relevant.

## Question bank rules

Generate:

- 20% factual recall of anatomy, physiology, drugs, definitions.
- 25% mechanism/pathophysiology explanation.
- 25% case vignettes and clinical reasoning.
- 15% investigation/data interpretation.
- 15% safety, ethics, communication, public-health, or medication questions.

Answers should include rationale and safety cautions, not only the correct option.

## High-yield exam prediction signals

Prioritize topics that:

- Are common, dangerous, examinable, or emphasized in cases/OSCEs.
- Link basic science to clinical presentation.
- Involve medication safety or red flags.
- Appear in tables, algorithms, guideline excerpts, or repeated case patterns.

## Active study plan

- Use active recall with blank-page disease scripts: definition, mechanism, presentation, tests, management principles, safety.
- Compare similar conditions in tables.
- Practice cases under time pressure and verbalize reasoning.
- Use spaced repetition for anatomy, drug classes, lab values, and diagnostic criteria.

## Scoring rubric

Award marks for accurate knowledge, mechanism reasoning, prioritization, data interpretation, safety awareness, patient-centered explanation, and recognizing limits of available information.
