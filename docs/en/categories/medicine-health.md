# Medicine / Health Sciences

Applies to medicine, nursing, pharmacy, dentistry, public health, epidemiology, anatomy, physiology, pathology, pharmacology, clinical skills, and related health science courses.

## Sub-discipline lookup

| Sub-discipline | Typical courses | Key question types |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic Medical Sciences | Anatomy, Physiology, Pathology, Pharmacology, Microbiology | Mechanism explanation, structure identification, drug reasoning |
| Clinical Medicine | Internal Med, Surgery, OB/GYN, Pediatrics, Diagnostics | Case analysis, clinical reasoning, test selection |
| Nursing | Fundamentals, Medical-Surgical, Critical Care Nursing | Nursing plans, priority judgment, patient education |
| Pharmacy | Pharmacology, Pharmaceutics, Medicinal Chemistry, Clinical Pharmacy | Drug mechanisms, adverse effects, medication monitoring |
| Public Health | Epidemiology, Biostatistics, Health Education, Health Law | Data interpretation, research design, policy analysis |
| Dentistry | Oral Anatomy, Endodontics, Prosthodontics | Case analysis, treatment planning, procedure steps |

## Safety boundary

Outputs are for course learning only and cannot substitute for medical advice, diagnosis, treatment plans, or professional guidance. For uncertain content, prompt verification against course materials, guidelines, and instructor-approved sources.

## Review goal

Help students connect normal structure/function, disease mechanisms, clinical manifestations, tests, management principles, medication safety, public health reasoning, and exam-style clinical thinking.

## What to extract from materials

- Anatomy, physiology, pathology, microbiology, pharmacology, and clinical skills frameworks.
- Disease definitions, etiology, risk factors, mechanisms, symptoms/signs, red flags, tests, and management principles.
- Drug classes, mechanisms, indications, contraindications, adverse reactions, interactions, monitoring, and patient education points.
- Clinical cases, OSCE checklists, nursing plans, epidemiological indicators, and public health interventions.
- Diagrams, tables, pathways, lab values, imaging descriptions, and course-provided guideline summaries.

## Chapter note structure

1. **System or disease overview**.
2. **Normal baseline**: Anatomy/physiology or public health background.
3. **Pathophysiology/mechanism**: Causal chain from risk factors to manifestations.
4. **Clinical manifestations**: Key symptoms, signs, red flags, distinguishing clues.
5. **Tests**: What each test looks for, how to interpret typical patterns.
6. **Management principles**: Learning-level choices, priorities, safety concerns.
7. **Pharmacology table**: Mechanism, uses, adverse reactions, contraindications, monitoring.
8. **Case reasoning template**: Assessment, priorities, rationale, next steps.
9. **Common errors**: Confusing similar diseases, missing red flags, unsafe assumptions.
10. **Case questions and short-answer questions**.

## Common question types

- Anatomy/physiology labeling and mechanism explanation.
- Compare similar diseases, drugs, pathogens, or public health measures.
- Interpret lab values, vital signs, epidemiological data, or case descriptions.
- Explain pathophysiological process from cause to symptoms.
- Pharmacological mechanism, adverse reaction, contraindication questions.
- Nursing priorities, patient education, ethics, safety, escalation.
- OSCE station planning or clinical communication.

## Clinical reasoning template

Use: **Presentation → Key findings → Differential diagnosis → Most likely explanation → Tests → Priorities → Safety/Red flags → Patient-friendly explanation**.

For each case, distinguish:

- Information directly given in the stem.
- Information inferred from the stem.
- Conclusions that cannot be drawn from the stem.
- Unsafe assumptions that should not be made.

## Question bank rules

Suggested ratio:

- 20% anatomy, physiology, drug, definition factual recall.
- 25% mechanism/pathophysiology explanation.
- 25% case and clinical reasoning.
- 15% test/data interpretation.
- 15% safety, ethics, communication, public health, or medication questions.

Answers should include rationale and safety prompts, not just the correct option.

## High-yield exam point signals

Prioritize:

- Common, dangerous, frequently tested, or case/OSCE-emphasized content.
- Content that bridges basic science and clinical manifestations.
- Content involving medication safety or red flags.
- Content appearing in tables, flowcharts, guideline summaries, or repeated case patterns.

## Active review methods

- Blank-page recall of disease scripts: definition, mechanism, manifestations, tests, management, safety.
- Comparison tables for similar diseases.
- Timed case practice with verbal reasoning chains.
- Spaced repetition for anatomy, drug classes, lab values, diagnostic criteria.
- Drug cards: mechanism → uses → ADRs → contraindications → monitoring.
- OSCE simulation: verbalize clinical communication and procedure steps.

## Common error-prone points

| Error type | Example | Prevention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Disease confusion | Type 1 vs Type 2 diabetes | Use comparison tables |
| Missing red flags | Ignoring chest pain danger signals | Check red flag checklist first |
| Drug confusion | Similar drug names | Memorize by class |
| Indicator calculation error | Incidence vs prevalence | Use formula cards |
| Nursing priority | Doing things in wrong order | Use ABCDE assessment |
| Mechanism jumps | Skipping intermediate steps | Draw complete causal chain |

## Scoring rubric

Points for: knowledge accuracy, mechanism reasoning, priority judgment, data interpretation, safety awareness, patient-friendly expression, recognizing information boundaries.
