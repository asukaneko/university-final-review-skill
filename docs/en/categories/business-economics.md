# Business / Economics

Applies to economics, finance, accounting, marketing, management, operations, entrepreneurship, business analytics, strategy, and organizational behavior courses.

## Sub-discipline lookup

| Sub-discipline | Typical courses | Key question types |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Economics | Micro, Macro, Econometrics | Graph analysis, model application, policy evaluation |
| Finance | Corporate Finance, Investments, Financial Markets, Risk Mgmt | Valuation, investment decisions, risk analysis |
| Accounting | Financial Acct, Managerial Acct, Auditing, Tax | Journal entries, statement analysis, cost calculation |

## Review goal

Organize business and economics materials into model selection, calculation, case diagnosis, evidence-based recommendations, and managerial judgment frameworks under constraints.

## What to extract from materials

- Models, frameworks, definitions, assumptions, graphs, formulas, decision rules.
- Financial statements, ratios, accounting rules, valuation methods, cost behavior, budgeting logic.
- Economic graphs, equilibrium analysis, elasticity, optimization, welfare, policy impacts.
- Marketing/strategy/management cases, frameworks, stakeholder maps, execution risks.
- Quantitative methods, data tables, dashboards, regression outputs, interpretation rules.
- Past case questions, calculation formats, scoring rubrics.

## Chapter note structure

1. **Decision problem**: What business/economic decision does this chapter support.
2. **Core framework/model**: Assumptions, variables, graphs, when to use.
3. **Formula and calculation table**: Symbols, steps, interpretation.
4. **Case application guide**: Which facts to extract, how to diagnose, how to recommend.
5. **Chart interpretation**: Axes, shifts, trends, managerial implications.
6. **Comparison table**: Models, strategies, financial ratios, market structures.
7. **Worked examples**: Numeric, graphic, case-based.
8. **Risks and limitations**.
9. **Common errors**: Confusing profit/cash flow, correlation/causation, average/marginal, strategy/tactics.
10. **Practice problems**.

## Common question types

- Define concepts and compare frameworks.
- Calculate ratios, costs, NPV, elasticity, equilibrium, forecasts, or journal entries.
- Interpret graphs, tables, financial statements, regression results, or dashboards.
- Analyze business cases and make recommendations.
- Evaluate strategies, market behavior, policy impacts, or organizational issues.
- Write brief memos or executive recommendations.

## Problem-solving templates

### Quantitative business/economics problem

1. Identify decision problem or model.
2. List data, assumptions, time horizon, units/currency.
3. Apply formulas step by step.
4. Interpret results: what they mean for the decision.
5. Sensitivity or limitation analysis.
6. If asked, provide recommendations.

### Case analysis

Use: **Situation → Problem → Evidence → Framework → Options → Evaluation criteria → Recommendations → Execution risks**.

### Economic graph problem

Use: **Initial equilibrium → Shock/Change → Curve shift → New equilibrium → Welfare/distributional impact → Policy implications**.

### Accounting journal entry problem

1. Analyze the nature of the transaction.
2. Identify affected accounts.
3. Determine debit/credit direction.
4. Check debit-credit balance.
5. Record entry with explanation.

### Financial statement analysis

1. Calculate key ratios (liquidity, profitability, leverage, efficiency).
2. Compare with industry benchmarks and historical data.
3. Interpret managerial implications of ratios.
4. Identify trends and anomalies.
5. Suggest improvements.

### Strategy analysis

1. External environment analysis (PESTEL, Five Forces).
2. Internal resource analysis (Value Chain, VRIO).
3. Strategic positioning (SWOT, Strategic Groups).
4. Strategy selection and evaluation.
5. Implementation recommendations and risks.

## Question bank rules

Suggested ratio:

- 20% concept/framework memorization.
- 25% calculation and graph interpretation.
- 30% case diagnosis and recommendations.
- 15% essay/policy/strategy evaluation.
- 10% comprehensive mixed.

Answers should include interpretation and managerial/economic implications, not just numbers.

## High-yield exam point signals

Prioritize:

- Content that links formulas/graphs to actual decisions.
- Content repeated in assignments, case discussions, exercises.
- Standard frameworks: SWOT, Five Forces, STP, 4P, NPV, break-even, supply-demand.
- Content with regular calculation formats or decision tradeoffs.

## Active review methods

- Build a model selection table: when to use each framework/formula.
- Practice calculations until modeling steps become automatic.
- Timed one-page case recommendations.
- Active recall of graphs and formula interpretations.
- Compare similar metrics and frameworks to avoid substitution errors.
- Framework exercise: given a case, analyze with 2-3 different frameworks.

## Common error-prone points

| Error type | Example | Prevention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Profit ≠ cash flow | Making money but no cash | Distinguish income statement and cash flow statement |
| Graph direction wrong | Shifting supply/demand backwards | Identify shock source first |
| Ratio inversion | Current assets/current liabilities reversed | Remember numerator/denominator meaning |
| NPV timing error | Beginning vs end-of-period cash flows | Draw timeline |
| Vague case recommendations | Suggestions not actionable | Be specific to action steps |
| Confusing average and marginal | Average cost = marginal cost | Draw U-shaped curve |

## Scoring rubric

Points for: model/framework selection, calculation accuracy, result interpretation, evidence use, recommendation quality, risk awareness, concise business expression.
