# Social Sciences

Applies to sociology, psychology, political science, international relations, communication studies, anthropology, criminology, social policy, and research methods courses.

## Sub-discipline lookup

| Sub-discipline | Typical courses | Key question types |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sociology | Intro Soc, Social Psych, Urban Soc, Economic Soc | Theory application, case analysis, social survey |
| Psychology | General, Developmental, Cognitive, Clinical Psychology | Experiment design, data analysis, case diagnosis |
| Political Science | Pol Sci Principles, Comparative Pol, Intl Relations, Public Admin | Institutional comparison, policy analysis, case study |
| Communication | Intro Comm, Journalism, Advertising, New Media | Media analysis, audience research, effects evaluation |
| Anthropology | Cultural Anthro, Social Anthro, Archaeology | Ethnographic analysis, cultural comparison, field methods |
| Public Management | Public Policy, Urban Mgmt, Nonprofit Management | Policy analysis, case diagnosis, management advice |

## Review goal

Connect theories, concepts, methods, empirical findings, cases, and policy implications so students can explain social phenomena and evaluate evidence.

## What to extract from materials

- Theories, theorists, models, constructs, variables, hypotheses, mechanisms.
- Research design, sampling, measurement, validity, reliability, ethics, statistical interpretation.
- Empirical findings, cases, policy examples, historical context, comparative evidence.
- Classroom debates, critical points, method limitations.
- Essay, report, case analysis, or data interpretation scoring criteria.

## Chapter note structure

1. **Core social problem**: What phenomenon to explain.
2. **Theory map**: Theorists, hypotheses, causal mechanisms, predictions.
3. **Concept definitions**: Constructs, dimensions, measurement indicators.
4. **Evidence summary**: Studies, cases, data patterns, limitations.
5. **Method notes**: Design, sampling, measurement, validity, ethics.
6. **Application examples**: Policy, organizational, media, community, cross-cultural cases.
7. **Comparison table**: Theories or methods side by side.
8. **Critique bank**: Strengths, weaknesses, biases, generalizability.
9. **Essay/case template**.
10. **Practice questions**.

## Common question types

- Define concepts and compare theories.
- Apply theories to cases, policies, events, or data.
- Evaluate empirical evidence and research methods.
- Interpret statistics, charts, questionnaire results, or interview excerpts.
- Write essays on debates, causes, consequences, or interventions.
- Design a small study and justify method choices.

## Problem-solving templates

### Theory application

Use: **Phenomenon → Relevant theory → Key concepts → Evidence/Case → Explanation → Limitations → Alternative theories**.

1. Phenomenon: What social phenomenon to explain.
2. Relevant theory: Which theories can explain it.
3. Key concepts: Core constructs in the theory.
4. Evidence/Case: Empirical studies supporting the theory.
5. Explanation: How the theory explains the phenomenon.
6. Limitations: Theory's shortcomings and criticisms.
7. Alternative theories: How other theories explain it.

### Research methods question

Use: **Research question → Hypothesis → Variables → Design → Sample → Measurement → Ethics → Validity threats → Interpretation**.

1. Research question: What question to answer.
2. Hypothesis: Theory-based prediction.
3. Variables: Independent, dependent, control.
4. Design: Experimental, quasi-experimental, survey, qualitative, etc.
5. Sample: Sampling method and representativeness.
6. Measurement: Operationalization and reliability/validity.
7. Ethics: Informed consent, privacy protection, etc.
8. Validity threats: Internal, external, construct validity.
9. Interpretation: What the data shows.

### Policy / Case analysis

Use: **Problem → Stakeholders → Causes → Evidence → Options → Tradeoffs → Recommendations → Evaluation metrics**.

### Critical analysis

1. Describe the core content of the study/theory.
2. Analyze its methodology and logic.
3. Evaluate its strengths and limitations.
4. Propose improvements.

## Validity threat checklist

| Threat type | Example | Prevention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selection bias | Non-random assignment | Random assignment |
| Measurement bias | Leading questionnaire wording | Standardized instruments |
| History effect | External events during testing | Control group |
| Experimenter effect | Researcher expectations influencing results | Double-blind design |
| Attrition | Unequal dropout across groups | Track attrition reasons |
| Social desirability | Participants answering to please | Anonymous questionnaires |

## Question bank rules

Suggested ratio:

- 20% concept and theorist memorization.
- 25% theory comparison and application.
- 25% research methods/data interpretation.
- 20% case or policy analysis.
- 10% comprehensive essay.

Answers should include evidence and limitations, not just theory descriptions.

## High-yield exam point signals

Prioritize:

- Theories that appear as core frameworks across multiple sessions.
- Content linked to classic studies, landmark cases, or policy debates.
- Content involving methods, validity, ethics, or data interpretation.
- Content applicable to current or unfamiliar examples.

## Active review methods

- Create theory cards: assumptions, mechanisms, evidence, criticisms, examples.
- Practice applying multiple theories to the same case.
- Redraw research designs and identify validity threats.
- Spaced repetition for theorists, constructs, and research findings.
- Write timed paragraph outlines for high-frequency essay verbs.
- Critique exercise: given a research design, find 3 validity threats.

## Common error-prone points

| Error type | Example | Prevention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Confusing correlation and causation | High correlation means causation | Check experimental design |
| Ignoring method limitations | Only stating strengths | Mandate limitations section |
| Vague theory application | Only describing, not analyzing | Connect with "because...therefore..." |
| Ignoring stakeholders | Policy analysis only one side | List all relevant parties |
| Data misinterpretation | Treating percentages as absolute numbers | Note base rates and proportions |
| Concept confusion | Using similar concepts interchangeably | Use definition cards to distinguish |

## Scoring rubric

Points for: concept accuracy, theory-case match, evidence use, method awareness, critique, balanced judgment, structural clarity.
