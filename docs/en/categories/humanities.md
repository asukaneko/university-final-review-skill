# Humanities

Use this guide for history, philosophy, literature, languages, cultural studies, religious studies, and related humanities courses.

## Review focus

- Key concepts, authors, schools, texts, events, and historical contexts
- Chronology, intellectual development, arguments, and interpretations
- Textual evidence, close reading, and thesis construction
- Comparison between ideas, authors, periods, movements, or works
- Essay planning and evidence-based argumentation

## Recommended chapter structure

1. Timeline or conceptual map
2. Key terms, figures, texts, and schools
3. Core arguments and historical context
4. Important passages, events, or examples
5. Compare-and-contrast tables
6. Essay answer templates
7. Common misreadings or oversimplifications
8. Practice essay prompts with outlines

## Typical question types

- Definition or identification questions
- Short-answer explanation
- Timeline or development questions
- Text interpretation
- Compare-and-contrast questions
- Essay questions

## Output emphasis

Help the student move from memorizing facts to building arguments. For essay questions, provide thesis statements, evidence points, analysis structure, and possible conclusion sentences.
